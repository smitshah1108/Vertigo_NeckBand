One frame, three systems working as one.

Sensing, feedback, and display are built as independent modules on the frame — so a failure in one never blinds the others.

Sense

MPU-6050 IMU

A 6-axis accelerometer/gyroscope reads head and body rotation at high frequency, capturing precise angular velocity and acceleration.

Think

Raspberry Pi Pico 2 W + Edge Impulse

A TFLite model trained on spin sequences runs entirely on-device, classifying balance state frame-by-frame with no round trip to the cloud.

Respond

ERM motor + optical HUD

A vibration driver returns a directional cue to the dancer's temple, while a combiner-mirror HUD — architected like a split Vision Pro display — shows a subtle heads-up correction.

From spin to signal in under a tenth of a second.

Every rotation the dancer makes moves through this chain, continuously, on-device.

Origin
Reframed around "Robots Meet Culture"
What began as a general motion-feedback band was rebuilt from the frame up around Indian classical dance — grounding every design decision in a real performance discipline rather than a generic fitness use case.
Build
Component sourcing under real constraints
Raspberry Pi Pico 2 W chosen for its dual ARM Cortex-M33 cores and low power consumption. Vibration driver current draw balanced against hot-swap LiPo runtime for full rehearsal sessions.
Validate
Model evaluation, honestly reported
Near-perfect training metrics were flagged, not celebrated — treated as a signal of possible overfitting or data leakage and re-tested against held-out rehearsal footage.
Now
Official WRO 2026 entry
Confirmed as Life Robotics' official Future Innovators submission. Documentation and reporting are in active refinement ahead of competition.
